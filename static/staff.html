Your daily desk

NyayaVedika's staff desk does two things first: case triage (which matters need the court soonest, and why) and efficient scheduling (a weekly cause list that fits the bench time). Everything else feeds these two.

How the Court Staff desk works · 4 steps, about a minute to read
  1. 1
    Receive papers

    FIRs, charge sheets, petitions and orders go in through Intake. Each paper is read, its facts are extracted with page references, and the processed cases move to the Docket.

    You: upload, and fill in any detail marked "needs review".

  2. 2
    Case triage Main focus

    Every pending matter gets a 0-100 priority from fixed rules, never from the AI: custody and the Sec. 479 BNSS mark, bail, time-bound statutes (e.g. Sec. 143 NI Act, DV Act), senior citizens, women and children, and how long the case has waited. The band (Critical / High / Medium / Routine) sets how soon it must be listed.

    You: check the cases at the top of the queue and correct any wrong fact; the score is recomputed.

  3. 3
    Efficient scheduling Main focus

    Prepare a weekly draft, check it, then publish. The draft lists must-list matters first (custody, bail, missed deadlines, cases left out three times), fits each bench's sitting time allowing for likely adjournments, and never books an advocate in two courts at once. Cases that do not fit are carried over and climb next week.

    You: check the draft, move or hold a matter if needed; the Registrar approves and publishes.

  4. 4
    Hear and record

    On the day, the Court diary below lists the matters. After the sitting, record heard / adjourned (reason and next date) / disposed / not reached. Adjournments feed back into triage and into how much time the next list allows.

    You: record proceedings; the court's order is always the authority.

Every day, separately: the checks custody matters against Sec. 479 BNSS. You record counsel on record (from the DLSA letter or vakalatnama); counsel decides whether to file; you record the filing, hearing and the court's order. NyayaVedika suggests and explains; the Registrar approves lists, counsel decides on filing, and only the court decides release.

    Undertrial Watch

    Custody cases from the Docket are checked every day against Sec. 479 BNSS. Anyone who appears past the mark gets an alert with a draft release application for a legal-aid lawyer. Uploaded and imported cases appear before synthetic examples.

    A calculation, not a decision. Each alert is arithmetic on the recorded custody date and maximum sentence. Periods of delay caused by the accused, other pending cases and the exact charge must be verified; a legal-aid lawyer approves any application, and only the court decides on release.

    Each alert moves through:

    1. Found
    2. Counsel decides to file
    3. Filed in court
    4. Heard
    5. Court's order
    6. Released
