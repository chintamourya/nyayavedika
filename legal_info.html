New: recently added rights

Rights people often miss. These are always shown first.

Held in jail before trial? You may have a right to be freed on bond

Law: BNSS s.479 · BNSS s.290 · BNSS s.293

  • A first-time offender, meaning someone who has never been convicted of any offence, must be released on bond by the court once they have spent one-third of the longest jail term for that offence in detention (BNSS s.479).
  • It is the jail superintendent's duty to apply to the court for your release once you reach that point. Even so, you or your family should ask the jail and your lawyer to work out your date.
  • The Union Home Ministry has asked all States and Union Territories to start applying this section for prisoners who qualify.
  • If you want to plead guilty, a plea-bargaining application has to be filed within 30 days of the charge being framed (s.290); a first-time offender may then receive only one-fourth or one-sixth of the punishment (s.293).
  • Free legal aid is available by calling 15100 (NALSA).

Crime victim? You can get a free FIR copy, updates on your case and free medical care

Law: Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023

  • As a victim, you have the right to a free copy of the FIR.
  • You must be told how your case is progressing within 90 days.
  • Hospitals are obliged to provide free first aid or medical treatment to victims of crimes against women and children.
  • Investigations into offences against women and children are supposed to be wrapped up within two months of the information being recorded.
  • When the offence is rape, the police must record the victim's statement on audio-video.
  • In an emergency, dial 112 (police, fire, health). The women helpline is 181 and the child helpline is 1098.

Women, children, seniors and people with disabilities: you are not required to go to the police station

Law: Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023

  • The duty to appear at a police station does not apply to women, anyone under 15, anyone over 60, or people with a disability or an acute illness.
  • For certain offences against women, the victim's statement should, as far as practicable, be taken by a woman Magistrate. Where none is available, a male Magistrate may take it with a woman present.
  • Call 112 in an emergency (police, fire, health), or 15100 for free legal aid (NALSA).

Your right to copies of your case papers (FIR, chargesheet, statements) within 14 days

Law: Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023

  • The accused and the victim are both entitled to receive copies of the FIR, the police report (chargesheet), statements, confessions and other documents within 14 days.
  • To stop hearings from dragging on, courts may grant no more than two adjournments.
  • Summons may be served electronically, and court proceedings may be held in electronic mode.
  • For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

Scared to testify? Witness protection is available

Law: Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023

  • Every State Government must have a Witness Protection Scheme, so that witnesses stay safe and are able to give evidence.
  • If you are a witness and feel threatened, report it to the police and the court, and ask the District Legal Services Authority (helpline 15100) how to apply under your State's scheme.
  • In an emergency, call 112 (police, fire, health).

Police, FIR and arrest

Reporting a crime, what to do when police refuse, and your rights if you or someone you know is arrested.

How do I report a crime and have an FIR registered?

Law: BNSS s.173 · BNSS s.531

  • Information about a cognizable offence can be given to the officer in charge of any police station, no matter where the offence took place. This is what is commonly called a "Zero FIR" (BNSS 2023 s.173(1)).
  • You can give the information orally, in which case it must be written down, read back to you and signed by you, or send it by electronic communication. An e-FIR is taken on record only once you sign it within three days (s.173(1)).
  • A copy of the FIR must be handed to you straight away, at no cost (s.173(2)).
  • When a woman reports certain sexual offences, the information must be recorded by a woman police officer or another woman officer (s.173(1), first proviso).
  • The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) took the place of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (BNSS s.531) and has applied since 1 July 2024.
  • In an emergency, call 112 (police, fire, health).

What can I do if the police refuse to register my complaint?

Law: BNSS s.173 · BNSS s.175

  • If the officer in charge will not record your information, you can post a written summary of it to the Superintendent of Police (SP) concerned (BNSS s.173(4)).
  • If the SP is satisfied that it reveals a cognizable offence, the SP must either investigate or direct an investigation. If that does not happen, you can apply to the Magistrate (s.173(4)).
  • A Magistrate empowered under BNSS s.210 can order an investigation on your application, supported by an affidavit (BNSS s.175(3); this is the affidavit mentioned in s.173(4)).
  • You can report at any police station regardless of where the offence occurred, and the informant or victim must be given a copy of the FIR immediately, free of charge (BNSS s.173(1), s.173(2)).
  • For some offences carrying three years or more but less than seven years, the officer in charge may first hold a preliminary enquiry, to be finished within fourteen days, to check whether there is a prima facie case. This needs permission from an officer of at least Deputy Superintendent of Police rank (BNSS s.173(3)).
  • For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

What rights does an arrested person have, whether it is you or someone you know?

Law: BNSS s.47 · BNSS s.48 · BNSS s.58 · LSA Act s.12 · BNSS s.531

  • Anyone arrested without a warrant must immediately be told the full details of the offence or the other grounds for the arrest. If the offence is bailable, the police must tell them they can be released on bail and arrange for sureties (BNSS s.47).
  • The police must promptly tell a relative, friend or other person named by the arrested person about the arrest and where they are being held, and must tell the arrested person that they have this right (s.48).
  • Unless a Magistrate specially orders otherwise, someone arrested without a warrant cannot be kept in police custody for more than 24 hours, not counting the travel time to the Magistrate's court (s.58).
  • Anyone in custody is entitled to free legal services (Legal Services Authorities Act s.12(g)).
  • The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) took the place of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (BNSS s.531) and has applied since 1 July 2024.
  • For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

Free legal help and settling disputes

Who gets a free lawyer, and ways to settle a dispute without a long court case.

Can I get a lawyer for free? Who is eligible and how to apply

Law: LSA Act s.12 · LSA Act s.13

  • You can get free legal services to file or defend a case if you are any of the following: a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe; a victim of trafficking or begar; a woman or a child; a person with a disability; a victim of a mass disaster, ethnic violence, caste atrocity, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster; an industrial workman; a person in custody; or someone whose income is below the limit (Legal Services Authorities Act s.12).
  • Each State may raise the income figure given in the Act.
  • According to NALSA, a woman gets free legal aid whatever her income.
  • An affidavit stating your income is normally enough to prove income eligibility (s.13(2)). The Authority must also be satisfied that you have a prima facie case (s.13(1)).
  • Free legal aid is provided through the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) in your district and the Taluk Legal Services Committees. NALSA's helpline is 15100, and you can apply online at nalsa.gov.in (Legal Aid Application).

Consult a lawyer over video at your nearest Common Service Centre

Law: LSA Act s.12

  • Tele-Law is a Department of Justice programme that links people to panel lawyers for legal advice by video or tele-conference, through Common Service Centres (CSCs), chiefly at the gram panchayat level.
  • A Government press release (PIB, Nov 2021) states that the consultation costs nothing for people entitled to free legal aid under Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, while everyone else paid Rs 30 per consultation.

Resolve a dispute at a Lok Adalat instead of going through a long trial

Law: LSA Act s.19 · LSA Act s.21

  • Legal Services Authorities organise Lok Adalats to resolve disputes through compromise (Legal Services Authorities Act s.19).
  • A Lok Adalat award has the status of a civil court decree. If it settles a case that was pending, the court fee already paid is returned (s.21(1)).
  • The award is final and binds all the parties, and it cannot be appealed (s.21(2)).
  • NALSA's 2026 calendar lists four National Lok Adalats, the fourth falling on 12 December 2026 (check with your DLSA or call 15100 for the date in your district).

Trouble with water, electricity, transport or other public services: the Permanent Lok Adalat

Law: LSA Act s.22A · LSA Act s.22C · LSA Act s.22E

  • The Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (LSA Act) s.22A(b) defines a "public utility service" as: carriage of passengers or goods by air, road or water; postal, telegraph or telephone service; supply of power, light or water to the public; public conservancy or sanitation; service in a hospital or dispensary; insurance service; and any other service the government notifies.
  • LSA Act s.22C(1): any party can approach the Permanent Lok Adalat BEFORE the dispute goes to any court. It has no jurisdiction over an offence that is not compoundable under any law.
  • LSA Act s.22C(2): once an application has been made, no party can take the same dispute to any court.
  • LSA Act s.22C(8): if the parties cannot agree, the Permanent Lok Adalat decides the dispute itself, provided it does not concern any offence.
  • LSA Act s.22E: its award is final and binding, counts as a civil court decree, cannot be challenged in any original suit, application or execution proceeding, and can be sent to the civil court for execution.
  • Permanent Lok Adalats can hear disputes worth up to Rs. One Crore. Approaching them is a compulsory pre-litigation step for conciliation and settlement of public utility disputes.
  • For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

Consider mediation before going to court

Law: Mediation Act 2023 s.5 · Mediation Act 2023 s.18

  • Under the Mediation Act, 2023, s.5(1): before filing any civil or commercial suit or proceeding in a court, the parties may, voluntarily and by mutual consent, try to resolve the dispute through pre-litigation mediation under the Act (commercial disputes of Specified Value are governed by s.12A of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015).
  • Mediation Act 2023 s.18(1): mediation has to be completed within 120 days of the date fixed for the first appearance before the mediator.
  • Mediation Act 2023 s.18(2): the parties can agree to extend this by a further period of up to 60 days.
  • For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

Women, children and family

Protection from violence and harassment, maintenance, and helplines.

Violence at home: protection, somewhere to stay and financial support

Law: PWDVA s.3 · PWDVA s.12 · PWDVA s.18 · PWDVA s.19 · PWDVA s.20 · BNS s.85 · BNS s.86 · BNS s.358

  • Domestic violence covers physical, sexual, verbal and emotional, and economic abuse, as well as harassment for dowry (Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005 s.3).
  • The woman herself, a Protection Officer, or anyone else acting on her behalf can ask the Magistrate for relief (s.12).
  • Through a protection order, the Magistrate can bar the respondent from committing violence, from entering her workplace, from trying to contact her in any way, and from selling or using shared assets and bank accounts (including her stridhan) without permission (s.18).
  • A residence order can prevent her from being thrown out of the shared household, whether or not she owns it (s.19).
  • The Magistrate can order monetary relief to cover expenses and losses, such as lost earnings and medical costs (s.20).
  • Women Helpline 181 is toll-free and open round the clock. Women also qualify for free legal aid.
  • Cruelty by a husband or his relatives is also a crime, punishable by up to three years' imprisonment and a fine (Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023 s.85; "cruelty" is defined in s.86 and includes harassment for dowry). The BNS replaced the Indian Penal Code (BNS s.358). You can report it to the police.
  • In an emergency, call 112 (police, fire, health). For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

How to claim maintenance for a wife, children or parents (BNSS s.144)

Law: BNSS s.144 · BNSS s.531

  • If a person with sufficient means neglects or refuses to support his wife who cannot support herself, his child who cannot support itself, or his father or mother who cannot support themselves, a Magistrate of the first class can order him to pay a monthly allowance (BNSS 2023 s.144(1)).
  • The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) took the place of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (BNSS s.531) and has applied since 1 July 2024.
  • For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

Elderly parents and senior citizens: seeking support from your children

Law: MWPSC Act s.4 · MWPSC Act s.5 · MWPSC Act s.9 · MWPSC Act s.23

  • A senior citizen or parent who cannot support themselves from their own earnings or property can apply for maintenance from their adult children. A senior citizen with no children can apply against a relative who has sufficient means and holds, or will inherit, their property (Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007 s.4).
  • The application goes to the Maintenance Tribunal and can be made by the senior citizen or by a person or organisation they authorise. The Tribunal may also take it up on its own (s.5).
  • The Tribunal can order a monthly allowance. The State Government sets the ceiling, which cannot go above Rs 10,000 per month (s.9).
  • If a senior citizen handed over property on the condition that they would be looked after, and that does not happen, the Tribunal can declare the transfer void if the senior citizen so chooses (s.23).
  • Elderline 14567 is the national helpline for senior citizens.
  • For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

Who to call for help: women and child helplines (181, 1098)

  • Women Helpline 181 is a free, round-the-clock service that offers support and information to women. It is run under the Ministry of Women and Child Development.
  • Child Helpline 1098 is the national helpline for children run by the Ministry of Women and Child Development (under Mission Vatsalya).
  • For any emergency, dial 112 to reach police, fire and health services.

Money and work

Unpaid wages, bounced cheques and consumer complaints.

My employer is late in paying my wages

Law: Code on Wages s.17 · Code on Wages s.45 · LSA Act s.12

  • The four Labour Codes, including the Code on Wages 2019, came into effect on 21 November 2025 and replaced 29 earlier labour laws.
  • Wages have to be paid on time: at the end of the shift for daily workers, before the weekly holiday for weekly workers, within two days after the fortnight ends for fortnightly workers, and before the 7th of the next month for monthly workers. If you are removed, dismissed, retrenched or you resign, your wages are due within two working days (Code on Wages s.17).
  • You, your registered trade union, or the Inspector-cum-Facilitator can bring a claim within three years before the authority appointed under the Code. It can order compensation of up to ten times the claim and should aim to decide within three months (s.45).
  • For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

What to do if a cheque you were given bounces

Law: NI Act s.138 · NI Act s.142

  • If a cheque issued to pay a debt or liability is returned for insufficient funds, the drawer commits an offence, punishable with imprisonment of up to two years, a fine of up to twice the cheque amount, or both (Negotiable Instruments Act s.138).
  • This applies only if all of these hold: the cheque was presented within six months of its date or within its validity period, whichever is earlier; the payee sent a written demand notice within thirty days of learning that it bounced; and the drawer failed to pay within fifteen days of receiving that notice (s.138 proviso).
  • The complaint has to be made in writing within one month from the date the cause of action arises (after the 15 days end). A late complaint can be allowed for sufficient cause (s.142).
  • For free legal aid, call 15100 (NALSA).

How to complain about a defective product, poor service or a refund

Law: CPA s.34 · CPA s.35 · CPA s.69 · CPA Jurisdiction Rules 2021

  • Start by registering a grievance with the National Consumer Helpline: toll-free 1915 (8 AM to 8 PM), WhatsApp +91 8800001915, or online at consumerhelpline.gov.in.
  • You can file a complaint about goods or services with the District Consumer Commission (Consumer Protection Act 2019 s.35).
  • The commission that hears your complaint depends on the value paid for the goods or services: up to Rs 50 lakh, the District Commission; above Rs 50 lakh and up to Rs 2 crore, the State Commission; above Rs 2 crore, the National Commission (Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021, made under s.34). You can file where the opposite party lives or does business, or where you live or work (s.34(2)).
  • A complaint must ordinarily be filed within two years of when the cause of action arose. A delay can be excused for sufficient cause (s.69).
  • You can also file online on e-Jagriti (e-jagriti.gov.in), which has absorbed the older e-Daakhil filing system.
