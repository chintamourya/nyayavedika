JUDGMENT

1. This criminal appeal is filed by the accused challenging the judgment dated 22.03.2021 passed by the learned Sessions Judge, Guntur in S.C. No. 118 of 2017, whereby the appellant was convicted for the offence under Section 304 Part II of the Indian Penal Code and sentenced to undergo rigorous imprisonment for seven years.

2. The case of the prosecution, in brief, is that on 05.06.2016 at about 8:00 p.m., a quarrel took place between the appellant and the deceased over the boundary of their agricultural lands. It is alleged that the appellant hit the deceased on the head with a stick, as a result of which the deceased fell down and was taken to the Government Hospital, Guntur, where he succumbed to the injuries on 07.06.2016.

3. On the complaint of the wife of the deceased (PW1), the police registered a case in Crime No. 76 of 2016 on 05.06.2016 and after investigation filed the charge sheet on 30.11.2016. The appellant was arrested on 06.06.2016 and has remained in judicial custody since then, except for a short period of interim bail.

4. Before the trial court, the prosecution examined PW1 to PW9 and marked Exs. P1 to P14. No defence evidence was adduced. The trial court relying on the evidence of PW1 and PW3, the alleged eye-witnesses, convicted the appellant.

5. Learned counsel for the appellant, appearing through the High Court Legal Services Committee, contended that PW1 and PW3 are interested witnesses and their evidence suffers from material contradictions. It was further contended that the medical evidence of PW7 shows that the injury could have been caused by a fall, and that the appellant has already undergone more than half of the sentence imposed.

6. Learned Public Prosecutor submitted that the evidence of the eye-witnesses is consistent and is corroborated by the medical evidence, and that the trial court rightly convicted the appellant.

7. The questions that arise for consideration in this appeal are:
(i) Whether the prosecution proved beyond reasonable doubt that the appellant caused the head injury to the deceased?
(ii) Whether the offence, if proved, falls under Section 304 Part II IPC or a lesser offence under Section 325 IPC?
(iii) Whether the sentence imposed is excessive in the facts of the case?

8. On point (i), we have carefully examined the evidence of PW1 and PW3. Though they are related to the deceased, their evidence is natural and consistent on the core aspects. Minor contradictions regarding the time of the incident do not go to the root of the matter. The relationship of a witness alone is no ground to discard the testimony, as held in Dalip Singh v. State of Punjab, AIR 1953 SC 364. We therefore hold that the prosecution proved that the appellant caused the injury.

9. On point (ii), the evidence shows that the incident occurred in a sudden quarrel, a single blow was given with a stick that was lying at the spot, and there was no premeditation. PW7 opined that the death was due to the head injury. In these circumstances, the case falls within Section 304 Part II IPC and the trial court has rightly so held.

10. On point (iii), the appellant has been in custody for over eight years including the period of trial. He is aged about 62 years and is a senior citizen. Considering the sudden nature of the quarrel, the age of the appellant, and the period already undergone, we are of the view that the ends of justice would be met by reducing the sentence to the period already undergone.

11. Accordingly, the criminal appeal is partly allowed. The conviction of the appellant under Section 304 Part II IPC is confirmed, but the sentence is reduced to the period already undergone. The appellant shall be released forthwith if not required in any other case. The Registry is directed to communicate this judgment to the Superintendent, Central Prison, Rajahmundry within three days.

12. Pending miscellaneous applications, if any, shall stand closed.
